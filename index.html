Add a real-time bird detection feed to your website, blog, or nature center display with a single line of HTML.

Copy & Paste

Drop this snippet into any webpage to show a live feed of recent detections:

<iframe
  src="https://joebarraco.github.io/BirdWatchAI-Releases/docs/widget.html"
  width="360" height="480"

Options

Customize the widget with URL parameters:

  • ?feeder=MyFeeder — Show detections from a specific feeder
  • ?limit=6 — Number of detections to show (max 20)
  • ?title=My Birds — Custom header text

Macro lens

A snap-on lens that tightens the shot for smaller visitors. Chickadees, wrens and finches fill much more of the frame, which makes for better photos and gives the AI more to work with.

It also throws everything behind the feeder out of focus. A neighbor, a passer-by or a car in the background comes out as a soft blur rather than a recognizable face — handy if the camera happens to look out over a sidewalk or a shared yard.

    Windows → macOS → Linux →

    git clone https://github.com/JoeBarraco/birdwatchai-server-releases.git ~/birdwatch

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What cameras are supported?

    BirdWatchAI works with any camera that supports RTSP streaming. We recommend the TAPO C113 for its excellent image quality and affordable price (~$30). Other popular options include Wyze, Reolink, and Amcrest cameras.

    Is my video feed sent to the cloud?

    No. Identification runs entirely on your own computer — your camera feed is never streamed anywhere, and there is no cloud service doing the recognition.

    Two things do reach the internet, and we would rather be precise than sound better than we are. If you set a location, the app fetches the temperature for it. And if you switch on community sharing — which is off until you turn it on — the sightings you choose to share are published publicly, including photos and clips if you enable those separately. Everything else stays on your machine. The privacy policy lists every outbound request.

    How accurate is the bird identification?

    Our AI model recognizes 965+ North American bird species with high accuracy. You can adjust the confidence threshold to balance between catching more birds vs. reducing false positives.

    Can I use this on Mac or Linux?

    Yes. BirdWatchAI Server runs on Windows, macOS, Linux and the Raspberry Pi — it installs with Docker and you use it from a browser on any device in your house rather than in a desktop window. Pick your platform above. If you'd rather not set one up at all, a preloaded Raspberry Pi arrives already running.

    Is there still a Windows desktop app?

    There is, but it's the old edition and it's no longer being developed. Everything shown on this page — the statistics dashboard, the species gallery, community sharing — is the server edition. Existing desktop users keep working and their license keys carry over; the last desktop release is linked at the bottom of the install section.

    What happens after the trial ends?

    The 30-day trial runs the whole product locally — detection, notifications, snapshots, clips, statistics and badges. The one thing it doesn't do is publish your sightings to the shared community feed; that comes with the license, because it stores your photos on our servers. Nothing is lost by waiting — sightings recorded during the trial stay in your history, and one button shares them all once you activate. When the trial ends, detection stops until you enter a license key. It's a one-time purchase, not a subscription, and your detection history and settings are waiting where you left them.

    Do I need an internet connection?

    Bird detection works offline! You only need internet for push notifications, email alerts, and weather data. The core functionality works completely locally.
